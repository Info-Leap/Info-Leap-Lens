# Respondent: Sunita Patil | Pune | Age 45 | Homemaker | Segment: Traditional Gold Buyer

## Background
Sunita manages household finances. Family income from husband's business. Holds significant physical gold (jewellery, coins). Has a basic bank FD. No stocks or mutual funds. First-time exposure to digital gold concept.

## Opening — Current Investment Behaviour
**Moderator:** How do you manage savings at home?

**Sunita:** My husband handles the business accounts, but household savings I manage. I keep some in post office savings — very safe. And we have a lot of gold. My mother-in-law's jewellery, some coins we bought for the children's future. On festivals we always buy some.

**Moderator:** How much gold do you estimate you have?

**Sunita:** Maybe 200-250 grams total in the family. Jewellery mostly. Some coins.

## Digital Gold Concept — First Exposure
**Moderator:** Have you ever heard of digital gold?

**Sunita:** No, what is that? Is it like an online thing?

**Moderator:** [Explains] You buy gold online, it's stored in a vault, you can sell anytime.

**Sunita:** But where is the gold? I can't see it? What if the company closes? This is risky no?

**Moderator:** It's regulated and stored with MMTC-PAMP or similar accredited vaults.

**Sunita:** I don't know these names. For me gold means I can touch it, keep it in my house or locker. Online gold feels like it's not real.

## Concept Exposure — CoinDCX Digital Gold with 5-7% Yield
**Moderator:** [Shows concept] What if your gold earned 5-7% per year?

**Sunita:** What does that mean? Like interest on gold?

**Moderator:** Yes, like your gold amount grows by 5-7% each year.

**Sunita:** That would be good. But why is someone giving me interest on my gold? Who is using my gold?

**Moderator:** Jewellers borrow the gold to make jewellery, pay you interest.

**Sunita:** Jewellers I understand. But I would want to know — is my gold actually there? Can I take it back as physical gold if I want?

**Moderator:** You can take it back as physical delivery.

**Sunita:** That makes me feel better. But delivery charges will be there? And making charges?

## Trust Barriers
**Moderator:** What is your main concern about this product?

**Sunita:** CoinDCX — I've never heard this name. What kind of company is this? If it's a new company, no. If it's like a bank or government, then okay.

**Moderator:** It's a registered company, SEBI regulated.

**Sunita:** SEBI I've heard. But it's not a bank. Can the bank offer this? HDFC or SBI?

**Moderator:** Not yet. CoinDCX is a financial technology company.

**Sunita:** Then I'd want to know — has anyone I know used it? If my neighbour or someone in my kitty party has used it and found it good, then I'd consider. Otherwise I won't risk it.

## Preferred Route
**Moderator:** [Shows Route 1 vs Route 2]

**Sunita:** Route 1 — "Your gold, always yours." This I understand. Route 2 with "earns more gold every year" — I like the idea but it sounds too good. I don't fully believe it.

## Final Advice
**Sunita:** This needs to come through someone I trust — a bank, a post office, or someone in my community. Marketing in apps won't work for people like me. Also, show me a clear certificate that my gold exists. If I get a paper saying "Sunita Patil owns 10 grams stored in XYZ vault," then I'll feel comfortable.
